Starting from Expressions (24.18) and (24.19), it is not difficult to write interpolation equations for the fringing field which are suitable for any values of $\gamma$ and $\vartheta$ in the interval $(0;\pi/2)$, but when $\phi=\pm/2$;
\[
\begin{aligned}E_\varphi=-H_\vartheta={}&\frac{iaE_{0\varphi}}2\bigl\{[F(2,\vartheta)F(1,\delta)f(2,\delta)\\&-F(1,\vartheta)F(2,\delta)f(1,\delta)]J_1(\zeta)\\&+i[F(2,\vartheta)F(1,\delta)f(2,\delta)+F(1,\vartheta)F(2,\delta)f(1,\delta)]J_2(\zeta)\bigr\}\frac{e^{ikR}}R,
\end{aligned}\tag{24.20}
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}E_\vartheta=H_\varphi={}&\frac{iaH_{0\varphi}}2\bigl\{[G(2,\vartheta)G(1,\delta)g(2,\delta)\\&-G(1,\vartheta)G(2,\delta)g(1,\delta)]J_1(\zeta)\\&+i[G(2,\vartheta)G(1,\delta)g(2,\delta)+G(1,\vartheta)G(2,\delta)g(1,\delta)]J_2(\zeta)\bigr\}\frac{e^{ikR}}R.
\end{aligned}\tag{24.21}
\]
Let us note that when $\gamma=0$ these expressions will be valid for any values of the azimuth $\phi$, since then any point of space may be considered to be located in the incident plane.

In the direction of the scattering diagram's principal maximum---that is, when $\vartheta=\gamma$, $\varphi=\pi/2$---the fringing field (24.20) and (24.21) takes the form
\[
\left.\begin{aligned}E_\varphi=-H_\vartheta&=\frac{ika^2}2E_{0\varphi}F(2,\gamma)F(1,\gamma)\frac{e^{ikR}}R\cos\gamma,\\
E_\vartheta=H_\varphi&=\frac{ika^2}2H_{0\varphi}G(2,\gamma)G(1,\gamma)\frac{e^{ikR}}R\cos\gamma.
\end{aligned}\right\}\tag{24.22}
\]
However, these expressions have an interpolation character, and with small values of the angle $\gamma$ it is impossible to consider them to be more precise than the simple equations of \S~9 and \S~12. In particular, with $\gamma=0$, when the fringing field must not depend on the incident wave polarization, they give values which are different for the E-polarization and H-polarization by small quantities of the order of $1/\sqrt{ka}$. Therefore, in this case (when $\gamma=0$) it makes sense to use Expressions (24.20) and (24.21) only far from the $z$ axis, switching to Equations (24.02) near the $z$ axis.
