The field arising with the diffraction of these waves by the wedge will obviously have the component
\[E_z=E_{0z}[u(r,\phi-\phi_0)-u(r,\phi+\phi_0)]\tag{1.25}\]
and
\[H_z=H_{0z}[u(r,\phi-\phi_0)+u(r,\phi+\phi_0)].\tag{1.26}\]
Let us find the integral representation for the function $u(r,\psi)$. For this purpose, let us use the equation (see [16], p.\ 866)
\[J_{\nu_s}(kr)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathrm I}^{\mathrm{III}}e^{i[kr\cos\beta+\nu_s(\beta-\frac\pi2)]}\,d\beta,\tag{1.27}\]
where the limits I--III mean that the integration contour goes from region I to region III (Figure 2). The cross-hatched sections in the plane of the complex variable $\beta$ $(\beta')$ shown in Figure 2 are regions in which $\operatorname{Im}\cos\beta>0$ $(\operatorname{Im}\cos\beta'<0)$. Therefore, in the sections of the contour extending to infinity the integrand strives to zero, ensuring the convergence of the integral. Substituting Expression (1.27) into Equation (1.20), we obtain
\[\begin{aligned}u(r,\psi)&=\\&=\frac1{2\alpha}\int_{\mathrm I}^{\mathrm{III}}e^{ikr\cos\beta}\left[1+\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}e^{i\nu_s(\beta-\pi+\psi)}+\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}e^{i\nu_s(\beta-\pi-\psi)}\right]d\beta.\end{aligned}\]
After summing the infinite geometric progressions and replacing the variable $\beta$ by $\beta'=\beta-\pi$, the function $u(r,\psi)$ acquires the form
\[\begin{aligned}u(r,\psi)&=\\&=\frac1{2\alpha}\int_{\mathrm I'}^{\mathrm{III}'}e^{-ikr\cos\beta'}\left[\frac1{1-e^{i\frac\pi\alpha(\beta'+\psi)}}-\frac1{1-e^{-i\frac\pi\alpha(\beta'-\psi)}}\right]d\beta'.\end{aligned}\]
