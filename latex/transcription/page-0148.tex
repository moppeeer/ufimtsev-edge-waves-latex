Now let us note that the expressions for the fringing field must satisfy the reciprocity principle---that is, they must not change with the simultaneous replacement of $\alpha$ by $\phi$ and $\phi$ by $\alpha$. Comparing Equations (21.21), (20.12) and (23.01), it is not difficult to obtain the expressions
\[
\left.\begin{aligned}E_z=-H_\varphi={}&E_{0z}[F(1,\varphi)F(2,\alpha)f(1)e^{ika(\sin\alpha-\sin\varphi)}\\&+F(2,\varphi)F(1,\alpha)f(2)e^{-ika(\sin\alpha-\sin\varphi)}]\frac{e^{i(kr+\pi/4)}}{\sqrt{2\pi kr}},\\
H_z=E_\varphi={}&H_{0z}[G(1,\varphi)G(2,\alpha)g(1)e^{ika(\sin\alpha-\sin\varphi)}\\&+G(2,\varphi)G(1,\alpha)g(2)e^{-ika(\sin\alpha-\sin\varphi)}]\frac{e^{i(kr+\pi/4)}}{\sqrt{2\pi kr}}.
\end{aligned}\right\}\tag{23.02}
\]
which satisfy the reciprocity principle, have no discontinuities anywhere, and are suitable for making calculations with any values of $\alpha$ and $\varphi$ ($|\alpha|<\pi/2$, $|\varphi|<\pi/2$). From the second equation of (23.02), it follows that $H_z=E_\phi=0$ when $\varphi=\pm\pi/2$---that is, the fringing field does not experience discontinuities in the plane $x=0$. Moreover, $H_z=E_\phi=0$ with any values of $\phi$ if $\alpha=\pm\pi/2$---that is, a plane wave polarized perpendicularly to the strip does not undergo diffraction with a glancing incidence.

The resulting Equations (23.02) may be investigated as interpolation equations. Actually, with $|\alpha|<\pi/2$ when $\sqrt{ka}\cos(\pi/4\pm\alpha/2)\gg1$ the functions $F(1,\alpha)$, $F(2,\alpha)$, $G(1,\alpha)$ and $G(2,\alpha)$ are close to one, and Equations (23.02) change into the previous Expressions (21.21) and (22.12). But if $|\varphi|<\pi/2$ and $\sqrt{ka}\cos(\pi/4\pm\varphi/2)\gg1$, then the functions $F(1,\varphi)$, $F(2,\varphi)$, $G(1,\phi)$ and $G(2,\phi)$ are close to one, and Equations (23.02) change into Equations (23.01). Let us recall that the functions F and G are determined by relationships (21.22), (21.23) and (22.13).
