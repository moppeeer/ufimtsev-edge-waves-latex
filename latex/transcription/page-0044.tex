Substituting series (4.03) into Equation (4.02) and then performing a term by term integration, we find the asymptotic expansion for the field radiated by the nonuniform part of the current. We limit ourselves to the first term of the asymptotic expansion, omitting terms of the order $(kr)^{-3/2}$ and higher. As a result, the required field from the nonuniform part of the current will equal
\[\left.\begin{aligned}E_z=-H_\phi&=E_{0z}f^1\frac{e^{i(kr+\frac\pi4)}}{\sqrt{2\pi kr}},\\E_\phi&=H_z=0\end{aligned}\right\}\tag{4.05}\]
with wedge excitation by plane wave (1.23), and
\[\left.\begin{aligned}H_z=E_\phi&=H_{0z}g^1\frac{e^{i(kr+\frac\pi4)}}{\sqrt{2\pi kr}},\\H_\phi&=E_z=0\end{aligned}\right\}\tag{4.06}\]
with wedge excitation by plane wave (1.24).

By calculating, with the help of Equations (4.05) and (4.06), the nonuniform part of the current, it is not difficult to see that it is concentrated mainly in the vicinity of the wedge edge. But the field created in the region $kr\gg1$ by this part of the current has the form of cylindrical waves, the angular functions of which are determined by the relationships\textsuperscript{(1)}
\[f^1=f-f^0,\quad g^1=g-g^0,\tag{4.07}\]
where in accordance with \S\ 1 and 3 we have
\[\left.\begin{matrix}f\\g\end{matrix}\right\}=\frac{\sin\frac\pi n}{n}\left(\frac1{\cos\frac\pi n-\cos\frac{\phi-\phi_0}{n}}\mp\frac1{\cos\frac\pi n-\cos\frac{\phi+\phi_0}{n}}\right)\quad\left(n=\frac\alpha\pi\right)\tag{4.08}\]
